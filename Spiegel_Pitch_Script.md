# Spiegel — 5-Minute Pitch Script

*9 slides, ~560 words. Timings assume a slower, deliberate pace (~100 words/min) — they add up to about 4:30, leaving ~30s of natural pause and breathing room so you land right at 5 minutes.*

---

### Slide 1 — Title (20s)

Every year, brands pour millions into campaigns — and some backfire the moment they go public.
I built Spiegel so you can catch that reaction before you launch, not after.

Spiegel tests a marketing campaign against a simulated audience — before you spend the budget.

---

### Slide 2 — The Problem: Two Examples (50s)

Here are two examples.

Gap, 2010 — the big American clothing brand. In October, Gap threw out the logo it had used for **over 20 years** — the classic navy-blue box every shopper knew — and swapped in this plain new design overnight. Customers hated it on sight. The backlash was so loud that Gap reversed course in just **6 days** and went back to the old logo — after a rebrand estimated to cost around **one hundred million dollars**.

Bud Light, 2023 — at the time, America's best-selling beer. In April, the brand sent a personalized can to trans influencer Dylan Mulvaney and posted it online. Many of its longtime customers were angry and stopped buying it — sales dropped by nearly **30 percent**, and Bud Light **lost its spot as America's #1 beer**, wiping out over **one billion dollars** in sales.

Neither was caught before launch. There was no way to see the reaction coming.

---

### Slide 3 — The Shift (40s)

Here's why this keeps happening.

Without Spiegel: you plan, you launch to the real world, and only *then* does the real audience react — the first genuine test, after the money's already spent.

With Spiegel, that reaction happens earlier — on a simulated audience, before launch. Same steps. One difference: *when* the real reaction happens.

---

### Slide 4 — How It Works (45s)

Under the hood, five steps.

Your brief and creative become a knowledge graph. That graph becomes buyer personas. Those personas react to your creative across simulated Twitter and Reddit. Every action gets logged and counted. And a report agent turns all of it into a verdict — one you can question, or interview directly.

---

### Slide 5 — What You Get (40s)

What comes back is everything you'd normally only learn *after* launch: reach, engagement, sentiment, virality, share of voice, round by round.

And it's grounded, not guessed. The knowledge graph runs in your own deployment. The metrics are counted from the action log. And the simulation runs on a real research engine — not one model's opinion.

---

### Slide 6 — The Business Opportunity (45s)

Why does this matter as a business?

Global ad spend is crossing **one trillion dollars** this year. And brands already pay for pre-testing — Kantar, System1, VidMob charge **$200 to over $10,000 a month** for surveys and panels.

The gap: those tools ask people what they think. Spiegel simulates what they'd actually do.

And it's more than marketing. Any question of majority opinion runs on the same engine — election polls, policy surveys, market research. Marketing is just the first use case.

### Slide 8 — Early Results (30s)

And here's how we check it's real. We crawled Reddit for the Xpeng P7+ and ran sentiment on **1,268 items** — 50 posts and over 1,200 comments. The verdict: net **-0.24**, clearly negative — **22% positive, 32% neutral, 46% negative**. Interesting split: the posts themselves lean positive, but the comments underneath are where it turns sour.

The simulation side is still running — but that's the whole test: when it finishes, we see how close Spiegel's simulated audience gets to this real reaction.
